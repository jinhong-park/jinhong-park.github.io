**Pick from 2026-10-12 (Oct 2 weeks)**



**Title:** Room-Temperature Metallic 2D Ferromagnetism Stabilized by Quantum Metric
**ID:** [2610.19713v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19713)
**Date:** 10/15 entry



**why this pick?**

This paper sits at the direct intersection of two of your core interests — **topological** (quantum geometry/metric) and **theoretical** — while delivering a clean magnetic result that is highly relevant to your solid-state theory program. It shows that the prominent quantum metric near topological band-crossing points can reconstruct the magnetic instability of a metallic 2D dice lattice and stabilize a uniform ferromagnetic order (with a predicted Curie temperature above room temperature) over the density-wave order that conventional nesting arguments would favor. Among the many entries this week, it is the one that most cleanly makes quantum geometry the *mechanism* that selects and stabilizes a magnetic phase, rather than a secondary diagnostic.

**1. Keyword matching across all six interests**

| Interest | How this paper connects |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Topological** | **Core.** The driving ingredient is the quantum metric (a fundamental component of quantum geometry) concentrated near topological band-crossing points at the K and K' points of the dice lattice. The quantum metric reconstructs the magnetic susceptibility, shifting its peak from finite-q (density-wave) to q = 0 and thereby selecting uniform ferromagnetism. |
| **Magnon** | Not directly treated — the paper is an electronic/itinerant result on the magnetic instability itself. However, the resulting metallic ferromagnet is precisely the host in which magnon spectra, Berry curvature of magnons, and magnon transport would next be analyzed; the quantum-metric mechanism that stabilizes the order is a natural starting point for those magnon studies. |
| **Phonon** | Not addressed. |
| **Theoretical** | **Core.** Pure theory: random-phase-approximation (RPA) analysis of the magnetic susceptibility plus Hartree–Fock mean-field theory, both incorporating the quantum-metric contribution, yielding consistent high Curie temperatures (well above room temperature). |
| **Skyrmion** | Not addressed. |
| **Altermagnet** | Not addressed — the order is ordinary ferromagnetism. The platform (dice-lattice electride YCl) is a conventional metallic magnet, not an altermagnet. |

**Only the pick makes quantum geometry (the topological metric) the active control knob that selects and stabilizes a magnetic phase.** 2610.21580 is an excellent magnon paper (linear spin-wave flat magnon evolving into a many-body continuum on an unfrustrated lattice that hosts ferrimagnetism via sublattice imbalance), but its topology is geometric rather than Berry/quantum-metric driven and it does not address the origin of the magnetic order itself. 2610.22309 and 2610.21854 both bring skyrmion/vortex textures; the former is experimental (3D-ΔPDF of polar skyrmion bubbles) and the latter is a first-principles + Heisenberg study of a vortex antiferromagnet that also hosts Weyl points. Both are valuable, yet neither engages quantum geometry or the mechanism that stabilizes order via band topology. The two altermagnet papers (2610.12900 and 2610.13584) are clean theory and directly hit the altermagnet keyword, but they concern excitons and charge-4e superconductivity, respectively, with no topological-band or magnon content. 2610.12557 is a beautiful theoretical construction of frustrated topological orbital magnetism (Weyl nodes, toroidal order, Hall responses), yet it is orbital rather than spin magnetism and does not involve the quantum metric. The pick is therefore the entry that most directly advances the quantum-geometry side of your program while remaining firmly theoretical and magnetically relevant.

**3. Physical insights from this paper**

- **Quantum metric as a magnetic selector**: conventional Fermi-surface nesting in the partially filled, weakly dispersive dice-lattice band of YCl would favor density-wave order; the large quantum metric near the topological crossings at K/K' reconstructs the spin susceptibility, moving its maximum to q = 0 and thereby stabilizing uniform metallic ferromagnetism.
- **Room-temperature scale from pure theory**: both RPA and Hartree–Fock calculations (the latter naturally incorporating the quantum-metric correction in the exchange) predict Curie temperatures well above 300 K, establishing the first concrete material platform for quantum-metric-stabilized room-temperature 2D metallic ferromagnetism.
- **Resolution of an experimental–theoretical tension**: ARPES shows a uniform flat-band splitting of ~0.5 eV; the quantum-metric mechanism explains why a uniform FM order appears instead of the density-wave state expected from nesting alone.
- **Conceptual bridge for your program**: the same quantum-geometry language (quantum metric, Berry curvature, band crossings) that appears here as the stabilizer of itinerant magnetism is precisely the language you use for magnon Berry curvature, symplectic geometry, and topological magnon bands; this paper supplies a clean electronic example of geometry selecting magnetic order that can be carried over to the magnonic setting.
- **Practical implication**: a room-temperature 2D metallic ferromagnet whose order is geometry-protected offers a route toward energy-efficient magnetic memories that does not rely on heavy-element spin–orbit coupling.



**TL;DR**

> This paper demonstrates that the quantum metric near topological band-crossing points can act as the decisive control knob that stabilizes uniform metallic ferromagnetism (with a predicted Curie temperature above room temperature) in the dice-lattice electride YCl, overriding the density-wave order favored by conventional nesting. It is a pure theoretical result that fuses **topological quantum geometry × itinerant magnetism**, making it the entry in this week's list most directly aligned with the quantum-geometry and solid-state-theory core of your research program.
