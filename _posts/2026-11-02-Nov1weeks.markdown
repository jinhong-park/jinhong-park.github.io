**Selection criterion:** With no explicit ranking among your six interests, I prioritize **the number of directly connected interests and the physical coherence of those connections**. This pick offers a theoretical path from magnon–skyrmion interactions to topological bands.

**3. Physical insights from this paper**

- **Magnon dispersion sets the pattern's length scale.** Interference between symmetry-related finite-wave-vector modes associated with a Mexican-hat dispersion produces the superlattice pattern.
- **The skyrmion helps select and excite the pattern.** It breaks translational symmetry and hybridizes internal modes with extended magnons, enabling excitation by a uniform AC magnetic field. The published paper also reports the same pattern under a local Gaussian drive without a skyrmion, clarifying this distinction.
- **Helicity provides an additional dynamical channel.** Breathing modes that are magnetically inactive in linear response can become weakly active through helicity dynamics.
- **The topological bands belong to the skyrmion lattice.** The paper reports nontrivial Chern bands inside the first magnon gap. Distinguish this lattice band topology from the periodic interference pattern around an isolated skyrmion.

These results are summarized in the [published abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/abstract/10.1103/fhbj-ykvn). The spin model and computational methods are described in the [preprint full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00363v1).

**Questions to explore while reading — my interpretation**

What hybrid modes could emerge if magnetoelastic coupling to phonons were added to these finite-wave-vector magnon patterns? Could a similar texture-assisted excitation mechanism operate in an altermagnetic background, and which symmetries would it require? These are possible follow-up research questions connecting your interests, beyond the results established in the paper.
