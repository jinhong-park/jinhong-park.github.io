**Title:** Equivalent Pseudo-angular Momentum Governs Selective Magnon-Phonon Hybridization  
**ID:** [arXiv:2610.34857v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34857v1)  
**Authors:** Hong Sun, Hao Chen, Lifa Zhang  
**Date:** 10/27 entry in the weekly collection; submitted to arXiv on 26 October 2026  
**Status:** arXiv v1 preprint; the authors report submission to PRL.

**why this pick?**

This paper uses crystal symmetry to explain **which modes can couple** when magnons and phonons meet in resonance. In a noncollinear stacked kagome model, hybridization is permitted only between modes with matching **crystalline pseudoangular momentum (PAM)**. Its general selection rule treats both collective excitations together, making it a useful theoretical tool for connecting your interests. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34857v1)

**3. Physical insights from this paper**

- **Symmetry compatibility governs mode coupling.** For an interaction preserving $C_n$, at a momentum invariant under that rotation up to a reciprocal-lattice vector, coupling requires $l_{\mathrm m}=l_{\mathrm{ph}}\pmod n$. Matching permits coupling but does not guarantee its strength.
- **PAM differs from local handedness.** It includes polarization transformations, sublattice permutations, and Bloch phases.
- **Energy and polarization can respond differently.** In the model presented, reversing the magnetic field changes the selected phonon's sense of rotation while preserving the hybrid-mode energies. [Selection rule and model results](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34857v1)

**Questions to explore while reading — my interpretation**

For an altermagnet, which magnetic symmetries remain, and at which momenta could the same selection rule apply? How strongly would previously forbidden modes couple if rotational symmetry were weakly broken? I see these as starting points for follow-up research connecting your interests; they are extensions to investigate beyond the results established in the paper.
