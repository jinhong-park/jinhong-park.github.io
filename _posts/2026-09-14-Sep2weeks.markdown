**Title:** Topological spin textures in 2D altermagnetic chromium chalcogenides: Interplay between magnetic frustration and Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction
**ID:** [2609.16780v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16780)
**Authors:** Zirui Zhang, Shiwei Zhu, Ruixiao Ma, Qiuyao Zhang, Xiaoping Wu, Changsheng Song
**Date:** 09/15 entry (arXiv 15 Sep 2026, cond-mat.mtrl-sci, 10 pp. 7 figs)



**why this pick?**

This week's collection (~230 entries) is dominated by quantum information / quantum-thermodynamics / active-matter noise; the condensed-matter core entries scatter across superconductivity, nickelates, and lattice models. Against that backdrop, 2609.16780 is the **only entry that simultaneously carries altermagnetism, band-adjacent topology, and a real topological spin texture (skyrmion family)** — it is the third axis of your research program (topology × altermagnetism × skyrmion) after 08/17 picked the insulator classification (2608.15811) and 09/07 picked the magnon spectroscopy (2609.09287). Using **first-principles + atomistic spin simulations** on monolayer and Janus monolayer Cr₂X / Cr₂XY altermagnets (X,Y = O,S,Se,Te), it shows that (i) strong intrinsic **exchange frustration** (NN vs further-NN competition) and (ii) a **Janus-activated in-plane DMI** (mirror breaking + heavy-chalcogen SOC) jointly drive a set of **non-collinear states**, and that a **GNEB / phase-space scan** reveals two competing mechanisms: *frustration lowers the critical DMI threshold* for non-collinear textures (synergy) while *frustration favors high-topological-charge states and DMI favors low-charge states* (competition). That "frustration vs DMI = charge competition" is the sharpest new design rule of the week and the most direct theoretical handle on skyrmion stabilization inside an altermagnet — exactly the missing piece between an altermagnet and a room-temperature, topological, skyrmion-hosting platform.

**3. Physical insights from this paper**

- **The skyrmion stabilization lever is *competition*, not just *DMI***: the familiar story (need DMI → skyrmion) is incomplete in altermagnets. Here **exchange frustration lowers the DMI critical threshold**, so a weaker DMI is enough — but the same frustration also *favors high-topological-charge states*, which DMI itself opposes. A skyrmion in a 2D altermagnet is therefore a **tuned balance** of two antagonistic knobs, not a phase you push into.
- **Janus asymmetry is the DMI switch**: mirror breaking in Cr₂XY (vs non-Janus Cr₂X) activates in-plane DMI *quantitatively* through heavy-chalcogen SOC — an explicit design rule that a single chalcogen swap (or a substrate field that breaks the mirror) is a skyrmionization knob.
- **Reading the three-week arc as one program**: 2608.15811 (which *symmetries* give a topological altermagnet) → 2609.09287 (what *spectroscopy* identifies a d-wave altermagnet) → **2609.16780** (how to *stabilize a topological texture* inside an altermagnet). This pick is the third leg: it takes the altermagnet you can already identify and asks what *texture* it will host, at what DMI and frustration cost.



**TL;DR**

> In monolayer and Janus Cr₂X / Cr₂XY 2D altermagnets, exchange frustration and DMI — activated by Janus mirror breaking and heavy-chalcogen SOC — jointly drive a family of topological spin textures (skyrmion-class). Frustration lowers the DMI threshold for non-collinearity (synergy) but competes with DMI on topological charge (frustration favors high-charge, DMI favors low-charge). A GNEB-verified design rule for skyrmion stabilization in 2D altermagnets — the third axis (topology × altermagnetism × skyrmion) of the arc that 2608.15811 opened and 2609.09287 extended.
