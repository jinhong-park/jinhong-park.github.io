**3. Physical insights from this paper**

- **Edge orientation selects the mass.** At zero added Zeeman field, axial edges remain gapless, while diagonal edges acquire masses from the altermagnetic term.
- **Alternating masses bind corner states.** The diagonal-edge mass sequence $+m_d,-m_d,+m_d,-m_d$ creates four Jackiw–Rebbi domain walls.
- **The relevant gap closing occurs at the edge.** In the leading edge theory, the model's Zeeman term shifts the masses to $J_0\pm m_d$; corner states disappear after the edge gap closes and reopens, while the bulk stays gapped.
- **Flux defects provide a complementary check.** The authors find zero-energy states at $\pi$-flux cores under periodic boundaries.

See [edge theory, flux response, and field dependence](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13438v2#S3). The flux result complements the boundary analysis; it should be read alongside the model's symmetries.

**Questions to explore while reading — my interpretation**

Could an analogous orientation-dependent mass mechanism be constructed for **magnon or phonon bands**, and which symmetries would protect the resulting corner modes? Could strain or a driven phonon tune the effective altermagnetic coupling? How would a spatially varying magnetic texture, including a skyrmion, change the mass-domain-wall picture? These are proposed research directions connecting your interests, beyond the results established here.
